Convoy TAMA-17, 29–30 April 1944

BANG's first attacks: four approaches on convoy TAMA-17 west of Luzon over the night of 29–30 April, with PARCHE and TINOSA closing in on her reports. Every mark is a position or attack typed into the boats' war patrol reports, beside the Japanese record of the same minutes.

a boat's reported positions, straight between them torpedo attack ship sighted, at the target's position hit (the Japanese record) the convoy's path as the boats saw it (ours)

Outcome, by the records

Japanese loss list: Takegawa Maru (1,930 GRT) and Nittatsu Maru (2,859 GRT), both to BANG. No loss matches PARCHE's or TINOSA's attacks next morning; the rest reached Manila on 2 May. (loss list, JANAC 4/p58; Nittatsu Maru's log, p23)

BANG's own count: "One 7000 ton AK probably sunk. One 8000 ton AO damaged" for Attack 1; "Sunk: None." (attack sheet 1, p16)

Who

BANG SS-385 · submarine · LCdr A. R. Gallaher
PARCHE SS-384 · submarine · LCdr L. P. Ramage; pack commander Capt. G. E. Peterson aboard

Convoy TAMA-17 Takao → Manila, sailed 1300 I on 28 April; 17 ships and escorts named in its record

When

29 April 0600 to 30 April 0800 GMT, 26 hours. BANG kept zone K (−10) and PARCHE zone I (−9); TINOSA kept zone H (−8) on station. Japanese records use Tokyo time (I, −9). On 29–30 April the target ship's own log and BANG's attack sheets agree to within one to seven minutes once both are in GMT.
