USS Haynsworth (DD-700) · TF 58 picket line · Kikusui No. 1

Twelve miles out: the kamikaze hit on USS Haynsworth, 6 April 1945

On the first day of Japan's mass kamikaze offensive at Okinawa, a destroyer posted 12 miles out from Task Group 58.3's carriers was hit by a single dive bomber that came out of low cloud. Seven men died at once; five more died of their burns. This page puts her own reports beside the other ships of the group and the Japanese records of the planes sent against them.

All times are Item (zone −9), which is also Tokyo time: the US Navy and the Japanese Navy kept the same clock here, so a 1250 in Haynsworth's report and a 1250 in a Japanese report are the same minute. Every mark on the map and every entry links to the page it was read from. Anything placed by assumption is hatched or dotted and says so.

1245 to 1250: the attack, as the action report tells it

Haynsworth's action report (9 April 1945, p. 3) describes the last five minutes in order but gives only two times, 1245 and 1250. These panels follow its wording. They are schematic: the ship's heading (020°) and the directions are from the report, but the distances are not to scale and the aircraft's exact paths are not recorded.

Silhouettes: JUDY 33 from TAIC's plate (CINCPAC-CINCPOA Bulletin 105-45, July 1945); F4U from FM 30-30 (April 1943). Hull outline schematic.

To scale: 100 feet

Irons has the Judy coming in at 100 ft and over 300 knots; the action report has it at 150 ft when the ship opened fire at 1,500 yards. Here are both heights beside Haynsworth, everything in feet at one scale, seen from her starboard beam, the side it hit. Head-on, as the starboard 40 mm crews saw it, the plane is drawn over the frame where it struck.

To scale: an estimated path for the last half-minute

Seen from above, to scale. What is recorded: the ship on 020° at 25 knots; the Judy heading away from her when she opened fire at 1,500 yards; its turn to starboard; the bomb falling about 1,000 yards abeam; the hit on her starboard side abaft the bridge, wings banked 45°. What isn't: where the plane was relative to the ship, how fast it turned, and how tightly. Those are the controls below, and the path follows from them: a turn's radius is fixed by speed and bank (r = v² / g·tan bank).

Where she was hit

The damage report (airmailgram 090010, action report pp. 7-9) and the war diary locate the hit by frame number: frames 65-88 of the main deckhouse, the radio transmitter room, Main Radio and CIC; the starboard bulwark at frames 70-79 "completely destroyed"; the twin 40 mm mount 41 "wrecked".
