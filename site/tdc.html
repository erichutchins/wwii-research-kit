The Torpedo Data Computer: its dials through one attack

An attack data sheet types what the fire control party set and fired: own course, the target's course and speed, the range, each torpedo's gyro angle and track. Between the periscope and the tubes stood the Torpedo Data Computer, which kept the target moving on its estimated course and worked out the gyro angle for every torpedo. This page turns a Torpedo Data Computer Mark 3's two faces, after its manual, to the solution the kit computes from a sheet, second by second, and says what each dial shows and where its value comes from. The torpedoes' tracks against the moving ships are on each engagement's page, under "At the periscope"; the triangle the computer solved is on Torpedo fire control.

What it was

The approach officer could measure the target's bearing, its range and, by eye, its angle on the bow; from a series of these the target's course and speed were worked out (USF 25(A) pp. 37, 49). The Torpedo Data Computer Mark 3 did that work in two parts: a position keeper, which kept the target's position moving on its estimated course and speed, and an angle solver, which worked the shot (OP 1056, Section 1: the position keeper's equations p. 22, the angle solver's p. 29). "The gyro angle, track angle and torpedo run are continuously being computed … The computed gyro angle is set on the torpedo continuously either automatically or by follow the pointer, spread angles being superimposed as desired" (USF 25(A) p. 50). "At the beginning of the war most submarines carried the Torpedo Data Computer Mark 3"; the Mark 4 followed, for radar data the Mark 3 "could be only partially utilized" with and for the Mark 18 torpedo (Rowland and Boyd pp. 399-400). The boats here are taken to have carried a Mark 3 (assumed: no record read names a boat's computer's mark; the Mark 3's manual was issued to boats "SS 167-544 incl." in June 1944, OP 1056 p. 277).

The faces below follow the manual's photographs: the Position Keeper (Fig. 98, p. 107) and the Angle Solver (Fig. 102, p. 111), each scale from its appendix (pp. 267-270), what the Target and Own Ship dials read from pp. 72-73, the Distance to Track indicator from Fig. 38 (p. 47). Each dial is an engraved disc turning under a fixed index at its top (p. 111): read every dial at its orange index. The lettering is drawn plainly, in the kit's type: the scans don't show the engraving finely enough to copy.

An attack

The Position Keeper

The left-hand face: the target's course and speed set in by hand, own course and speed from the ship, and the target's bearing and range kept moving from them. The numbers key the dials to the list.

    The Angle Solver

    The right-hand face: the gyro angle each side's next tube is ordered, its spread offset and the torpedo's run, from the position keeper's bearing and range and the torpedo's speed. The after half is on the left, the forward on the right; the side without a tube to fire on this sheet stands dimmed.
