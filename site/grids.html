Map grids: reading the Army's grid references

The morning reports and the battalion journals place a battery with a grid reference: V-044572, RN 25, Q9435. Between Sicily in 1943 and Bavaria in 1945, the Army units in these records used six different grids. Their square letters overlap, so a reference only means a place once you know which grid it is on.

How to read one

V-044572

Battery D, 26 September 1944: "1 9/10 mi No of Epinal, France (V-044572)". V is a 100 km square. 044 is the easting: 04.4 km east of the square's west edge. 572 is the northing: 57.2 km north of its south edge. Six digits give a 100 m square, four give 1 km (Q-8623), two give 10 km (RN 25). The easting always comes first.

A grid is a flat map's own coordinate system, in metres, drawn over the sheet. All six used here are Lambert conformal conic projections, each centred on its own region. Each is cut into 100 km squares lettered A to Z without I, five to a row from the north-west, so the same letters come round again in every grid and in every 500 km block. Battery D's J in August 1944 is near Grenoble. Its J in November is 300 km north, in the Haute-Saône. N is Naples, Montélimar or the Spessart, depending on the grid. The map sheet named its grid in the margin. The typist didn't, so the grid has to be worked out from the date and the towns.

The grids changed with the map sheets, not on any one date. In mid-September 1944 Battery D was writing Lambert II references in the Doubs (P-074873, 14 Sep). Six days later, in the Vosges, it wrote Nord de Guerre (V-032425, 20 Sep). In November it was back on a Lambert II sheet for one move (J-762365, Passavant-la-Rochère), then on Nord de Guerre again in Alsace. The 894th's medical detachment wrote the 500 km block letter as well (VZ-6457, Contrexéville). Battery D never did.

Try one

A reference without a 500 km letter is read in the block these records use: South Italy's r, the Lambert zones' only block, Nord de Guerre's eastern block (type VZ… for the western one).

How each grid was identified

None of the entries says which grid it uses, except the 62nd's Sicilian ones ("1:25,000 South Italy Grid"). Each reference was decoded on every grid and checked against the town the same entry names. The right grid puts it within a few kilometres of the town. A wrong one puts it hundreds of kilometres away. The tables below give every reference and how far it falls from the named town, worked out as the page loads. The towns are taken at their centres, so a kilometre or two is as close as the check can get. The grid each reference was found to be on is recorded in the data, in the grid_on column of records/army/stations.csv and actions.csv.

A map of these stations should use whichever position is more exact. A reference to 1 km or better that falls within 8 km of its named town belongs at the grid point: it is the record's own position, finer than a town centre. A two-digit reference, good only to 10 km, leaves the station at the town. So does a reference that misses its town, which is a typing slip.

  • Nord de Guerre: parameters as in tools/ndg_grid.py, used for the 1945 references. West of 400 km east, the squares belong to the western 500 km block, lettered V. The medical detachment's clerk typed that letter; the 128th AAA's HQ Battery didn't, crossing the block from Étampes (W-882948) to Nancy (U-854103) in August–September 1944.
  • Lambert I: origin 49.5°N (55 grad) on the Paris meridian, scale 0.99987734, the same false origin as II and III. Its letters sit in a block from 0 km east and 0 km north, lettered V: Co. B, 313th Infantry's VT 1099 (Le Valdécie), VT 0395 (Barneville) and VT 1286 (Baudreville) each fall within about a kilometre of their villages. HQ Battery, 128th AAA typed the square alone, and its references bear out the layout two rows further south and east: O-145180 (Cherbourg), Y-163828 (Romazy, 0.7 km) and Z-374373 (Le Mans, 3 km).
  • Lambert II and III: the standard parameters of the French zones, with their original false northing of 200 km (today's "Lambert II étendu" and "Lambert III" add 2,000 or 3,000 km). The wartime square letters were not found written down. These letters, for the 500 km block from 500 to 1,000 km east and 0 to 500 km north, fit every lettered reference here: U, N and J in Zone III; P, Y, O and J in Zone II.
  • South Italy Zone: projection and 500 km letters from notes on the wartime "Modified British" grids. The 100 km letters follow the British pattern, which every reference here fits.
  • Assumed: Battery D's August 1944 references have no letter. They fit the Lambert zones, not Nord de Guerre, but they can't show which square or which zone. Each is put in the square nearest the named town, on the zone the town is in (hollow dots). The Sicilian references give a sheet number instead of a letter and are placed the same way.
  • North Italy Zone: projection from sigterritoires.fr (origin 45.9°N 14°E, scale 0.9989, false easting 800 km, false northing 601 km). It reproduces a worked reference on a 1944 Ancona sheet (Camerata Picena, 477434) to 100 m. Its letters are not laid out like South Italy's. The index printed on a Bologna–Ravenna sheet puts square M at 600–700 km east and 400–500 km north, so the 500 km block runs from 500 km east and 200 km north. Laid out the South Italy way, the letters come out 200 km off: Siena would be F, not the Q the detachment's clerk typed. Laid out from the sheet's index, both references fall in Q. Siena's WQ 81 lands on the city, digits and all. Frontignano's WQ 71 is the 10 km square just north of the hamlet of that name (Murlo), the only Frontignano near Siena. The hamlet is 4 km beyond the square's southern edge. The layout has been checked only against that sheet index and these two references, which share one square.

Two-digit references are only good to 10 km, so they miss their towns by up to about 8 km. Of the finer ones, ten miss by 10 km or more. Five are in the 1945 journal, and the notes in records/army/stations.csv explain them: a slipped digit (Eisenberg, Osthofen, Ober-Sailauf, Weißenburg), or a typist who kept the old square letter after the battalion crossed into the next square (Gersfeld, N-550079 for H-550079). The sixth, Battery D's N823076 of July 1944, falls 13 km from "10 Mi. SE Battipaglia" and is unexplained. The other three are Co. B, 313th Infantry's in Normandy, 5–11 July 1944: its clerk kept typing square O, the Cherbourg square, after the company moved south into T. Read as VT, all three fall beside the hamlets they name. The tenth is the same slip a month later: the 128th AAA's HQ Battery typed Romazy's square Y for "near Rennes" (Y-977547); in X, the square to the west, it falls 3.4 km from the city. Hover a dot to read its note.

Times have letters too

The journals time their entries the way the Navy did, with a zone letter: 1600A is GMT+1, 1200B GMT+2. The 106th's records change from B to A in mid-September 1944 and back to B in April 1945. See the Pacific page on zone letters for the system.
