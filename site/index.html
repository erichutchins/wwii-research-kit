Pacific

Three submarines and a convoy, 29–30 April 1944

BANG, PARCHE and TINOSA hunted convoy TAMA-17 through the Luzon Strait as a wolfpack. Their three patrol reports kept three different clocks, and the convoy's own record kept Tokyo time. Put on one clock, they tell the same night from both sides.

One hit, four times: USS Haynsworth, 6 April 1945

On the first day of the mass kamikaze attacks at Okinawa, a single dive bomber hit a destroyer on picket duty twelve miles out from the carriers. Seven men died at once. Her radio was wrecked, so the records around her logged the hit when the news reached them.

Times are Item (zone −9), which was also Tokyo time: the two navies' 1250s are the same minute.

A battalion in pieces: the 20th Seabees, 1942–1945

A construction battalion rarely stayed in one place. The 20th crossed the Pacific in two waves, split into echelons and detached companies across the Solomons and New Guinea, and came together again on Saipan and Okinawa. Its muster rolls follow every part.

Blue lines are parts of the battalion; the black line is the battalion together. Point at a part to see where it went.

North Africa and Europe

Five minutes over Neuburg, 26 April 1945

Me 262 jets attacked a formation of B-26s over the Danube. An anti-aircraft battalion fired on them, a field artillery battalion watched from below, and the bomb group lost three aircraft. None of the three records mentions the others.

The Army's B time and German summer time were both UTC+2: no conversion.

Do this yourself

Everything here was found in public archives and can be done again for another ship or unit. The kit's tools are written to be run by a person or by an AI agent such as Claude Code, under one rule before all others: cite every source.

Find the records

War patrol reports, war diaries and Missing Air Crew Reports are scanned in the National Archives catalog; Army morning reports and Navy muster rolls are on its microfilm. tools/nara.py, morning_reports.py and jacar.py search them.

Transcribe what was typed

Every value exactly as the page has it, with the page it came from. A correction goes beside the typed value with its reason, never over it.

Check it

check_track.py flags a position a ship couldn't have reached from the one before; check_sources.py checks that every row's citation resolves.

Send it

Open a pull request to the records. The kit's Claude Code skills do the first three steps with you: transcribe-patrol-report, transcribe-morning-reports, find-the-other-side and add-silhouette.

Found a mistake, or have a lead?

Corrections and leads are welcome as GitHub issues. You'll need a free GitHub account; then report a correction (which mark, and what the page says instead) or share a lead (a ship or unit named in a family's papers, and what the papers say). The papers stay yours; only the unit and its records come here, never a person's name.
