The engagements

An engagement is two sides meeting at a place and a time, each side in its own records, on its own clock. Each page puts both on one map and one clock: a convoy battle from three submarines' reports and the escort's diary; a destroyer's day from her action report and the air group's; a bridgehead from a corps' report under the army group's situation map of each day. Every mark opens the page it was read from, and the questions the records leave open are answered source by source behind it. The index of every engagement.

Every subject

Each boat, ship, Army unit, Seabee battalion and convoy the records follow has a page of its own: what it was, its journey in stages, and its own map with every entry, each opening the page it was read from; behind it, how each position was placed and what is assumed. The index of every subject.

Do this yourself

Everything here was found in public archives and can be done again for another ship or unit. The kit's tools are written to be run by a person or by an AI agent such as Claude Code, under one rule before all others: cite every source.

Find the records

War patrol reports, war diaries and Missing Air Crew Reports are scanned in the National Archives catalog; Army morning reports and Navy muster rolls are on its microfilm. tools/nara.py, morning_reports.py and jacar.py search them.

Transcribe what was typed

Every value exactly as the page has it, with the page it came from. A correction goes beside the typed value with its reason, never over it.

Check it

check_track.py flags a position a ship couldn't have reached from the one before; check_sources.py checks that every row's citation resolves.

Send it

Open a pull request to the records. The kit's Claude Code skills do the first three steps with you: transcribe-patrol-report, transcribe-morning-reports, find-the-other-side and add-silhouette.

Found a mistake, or have a lead?

Corrections and leads are welcome as GitHub issues. You'll need a free GitHub account; then report a correction (which mark, and what the page says instead) or share a lead (a ship or unit named in a family's papers, and what the papers say). The papers stay yours; only the unit and its records come here, never a person's name.
